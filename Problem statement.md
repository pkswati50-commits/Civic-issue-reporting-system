Problem Statement 19
Crowdsourced Civic Issue Reporting and Resolution System
1. Problem Overview

In many cities and towns, citizens encounter civic issues on a daily basis, such as potholes, damaged roads, overflowing garbage bins, broken streetlights, water leakage, drainage blockages, illegal dumping, and other public infrastructure problems.

Although citizens may want to report these issues, the existing reporting process is often fragmented, slow, and difficult to track. Complaints may be submitted through different channels such as phone calls, municipal offices, websites, social media, or messaging platforms. This makes it difficult for authorities to maintain a centralized view of civic problems and prioritize them effectively.

At the same time, citizens often have limited visibility into what happens after a complaint is submitted. They may not know whether their complaint was verified, assigned to the appropriate department, acted upon, or actually resolved.

This creates a gap between citizen reporting and effective civic resolution.
Core Gap

The problem is no longer simply how citizens can report civic issues. The real challenge is ensuring that every reported issue is verified, intelligently prioritized, assigned to the right authority, effectively resolved, independently validated, and analyzed for future prevention.

2. Core Problem

The core challenge is to develop a centralized, transparent, intelligent, and crowdsourced civic issue reporting and resolution platform that connects citizens with the appropriate authorities.

The system should allow citizens to easily report civic issues using information such as:

Issue description
Photograph or video evidence
Geographic location
Issue category
Severity or urgency
Date and time of reporting

The platform should then help authorities verify, prioritize, assign, track, resolve, and evaluate these complaints through a structured workflow.

The objective is not simply to create another complaint-submission portal.

The system should provide end-to-end issue lifecycle management:

Report → Verify → Prioritize → Assign → Work → Resolve → Verify → Learn

3. Why This Problem Matters

Unresolved civic issues can directly affect public safety, accessibility, sanitation, transportation, infrastructure, and quality of life.

For example:

A single pothole may initially appear to be a minor road-maintenance issue. However, if it remains unresolved, it can contribute to traffic disruption, vehicle damage, accessibility problems, and potential accidents.

Similarly, repeated garbage accumulation in a particular location may indicate a deeper problem involving waste collection frequency, route planning, infrastructure, or lack of monitoring.

Therefore, civic issue management should move beyond simply counting complaints.

Authorities should be able to understand:

Where problems are occurring
What types of problems are most common
Which problems are urgent
Which departments are responsible
How long issues take to resolve
Which locations repeatedly experience the same issue
Whether citizens consider the resolution satisfactory
Whether previously resolved issues are recurring or reopening
4. Existing Challenges
4.1 Fragmented Reporting Channels

Citizens may use different channels to report problems, resulting in complaints being scattered across multiple systems.

4.2 Lack of Centralized Information

Authorities may not have a single dashboard showing all active, pending, assigned, and resolved civic issues.

4.3 Poor Prioritization

When many complaints are received simultaneously, authorities may struggle to determine which issues require immediate attention.

A broken streetlight near a public facility, for example, may require faster attention than a low-impact maintenance request.

4.4 Limited Transparency

Citizens frequently lack visibility into the status of their complaints after submission.

4.5 Duplicate Complaints

Multiple citizens may report the same issue separately, resulting in duplicate records and inefficient resource allocation.

4.6 Delayed Department Assignment

A complaint may need to pass through multiple levels before reaching the appropriate department, increasing resolution time.

4.7 Lack of Resolution Verification

Marking an issue as "resolved" does not necessarily mean that the problem has actually been fixed.

4.8 No Long-Term Learning

Historical complaint information is often underutilized.

Without analyzing past issues, authorities may miss recurring patterns and opportunities for preventive action.
